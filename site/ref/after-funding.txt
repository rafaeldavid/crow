# Crow reference: after funding

What either party can do once a deal is funded, besides waiting for the adjudicator.

## Beyond the happy path

Each is prepared, verified and submitted as `ref/signing.txt`, "Prepare, verify, sign, submit", says.

- **Early accept (buyer, only in `Delivered`).** `POST /v1/prepare/early-accept {"commitmentId", "nonce"}`; one slot, `EarlyAccept {commitmentId, buyer, attempt, deliveryHash, nonce, expiresAt}`; submit to `/v1/commitments/<id>/accept`. The seller is paid now, your key is released to you, the adjudication fee comes back to you, and the deal is final. So before you sign, open your own envelope: get the seller's signed delivery from the seller, check its delivery hash equals `payload.deliveryHash` of `delivery.submitted` at both operators, open your envelope, decrypt, and check `plaintextSha256`. Then check `deliveryHash` in the message is that hash: put the hash of the delivery you opened there, and the contract refuses the accept relayed with any other delivery. If anything fails, wait for the adjudicator instead.
- **Settlement by agreement (both parties, in `Held`, `Delivered`, `FallbackJudging` or `Escalated`).** `POST /v1/prepare/settlement {"commitmentId", "nonce", "toSellerBps"}` (or `payouts: [{"to", "amount": {"token", "amount"}}]` summing to the price, each `to` the buyer's payment address or the seller's payout address, the only two the phase-one contracts pay; optional `releaseKey`, `expiresAt`). Both sign the one `MutualSettlement {commitmentId, hasDelivery, deliveryHash, payouts, releaseKey, nonce, expiresAt}` (slots `buyerSignature` and `sellerSignature`); either submits to `/v1/commitments/<id>/settlements`. The adjudication fee returns to the buyer.
- **Settling late work (both parties, in `Held`).** When no delivery is posted, for example because the work came after `deliverBy`, the settlement can name the delivery the buyer holds: send the seller's signed delivery as `delivery` in the prepare call, and the settlement releases its key to the buyer. `releaseKey` needs a delivery, and the delivery needs an envelope for the buyer. The operators' reads do not show a late delivery's hash, so keep the signed delivery and the settlement as your record.
- **Appeal: not in phase one.** The contracts on Sepolia take no party appeal: `POST /v1/prepare/appeal` and `POST /v1/commitments/<id>/appeals` answer 409 `invalid_state` with the reason, and the first decision by the primary or the fallback adjudicator is final. The full protocol's appeal, with its bond, runs on SimChain only (`pnpm demo`).
- **Submitting directly to the contract.** These operators read the contract's logs and show your step within a minute, with `submittedVia: "direct"`. The contract keeps only a delivery's hash, so after a direct `deliver` also POST the same signed delivery to `/v1/commitments/{id}/deliveries`, at once or later. The operator joins it with no second delivery on chain and hands the adjudicators their keys; without that post they cannot open the work. If the operator cannot read your transaction yet, it answers 409 `invalid_state` and says it keeps your copy. It joins the copy within about a minute, and a retry with the same `Idempotency-Key` then answers `Delivered`.
- **Deadlines close deals on their own.** Once a deadline passes, the operators send its timeout at their own gas. They wait a minute, so you can send it first, and send it at their next pass: one to two minutes after the deadline in all. The contract checks each before they send it. They send:
  - `reclaim`, after `deliverBy` with nothing delivered: the buyer gets price and fee back;
  - the default, after `fallbackBy` with no fallback decision, or after `appealDecideBy` for an escalation: the buyer gets price and fee back. The live service pays the seller nothing on a default; bond compensation is the full protocol on SimChain only (`docs/architecture.md`).

  An offer nobody funded expires at `offerExpiresAt` by the operators' clock: their own record, with no transaction, and from then the contract refuses to fund it. They do not send the fallback takeover before `fallbackBy`, which is the fallback adjudicator's turn, and they cap how many they send a day. An early accept or a settlement by agreement after a deadline has only those one to two minutes: sign it before.
- **Your own trigger (anyone, no signature).** You need not wait for the operators: `POST /v1/commitments/<id>/triggers/<kind>` with `{}` once the deadline has passed (`reclaim`, `fallback`, `default`, `apply-default`). On the phase-one contracts `default` refunds price and fee in one call, so `apply-default` then has nothing to send; `appeal-expiry` is not in phase one and answers 409 `invalid_state`. Too early is 409 `invalid_state`. "Passed" is by the chain's clock: the contract compares the deadline with the timestamp of the block the call lands in, which can trail your machine's clock and discovery's `settlementTime` by a block or more. A `reclaim` sent 2 s after `deliverBy` can answer 409 `invalid_state` whose detail names `TooEarly()`; wait for discovery's `settlementTime` to pass the deadline, then retry every 10 s or so. Once this node has recorded the timeout, whoever sent it, the call answers 200 with the state it left and sends nothing. A deal behind the chain (`ref/limits.txt`, "Honest limits"), or a timeout you sent straight to the contract before the operators read it (within about a minute), still gets the contract's 409.

## A record anyone can check offline

A public deal closed by an adjudicator's accept or reject leaves one file anyone can check with no network. In a clone of [the repository](https://github.com/rafaeldavid/holdback), after `pnpm install`:

- `node examples/evidence.ts fetch <commitment ID> --out deal.json` writes the evidence bundle: the operator's terms, events, receipts and judgment record, the encrypted work, and the raw signed transactions (the buyer's agreement, the seller's delivery and the adjudicator's decision are only in their calldata). Env: `HOLDBACK_OPERATOR`, `SEPOLIA_RPC_URL`.
- `node examples/evidence.ts check deal.json` checks it offline: it runs the deal record's own checks over the file (the record of `buy.txt` step 11, which runs them live), then the signed messages inside each transaction, and the decision and the judgment record against the terms. It prints what that proves and the one chain read it cannot make, `eth_getTransactionReceipt` of the decide transaction, as a command; add `--receipt <answer file>` to check that answer too. Exit 0 passed, 1 a check failed (do not rely on the file), 2 could not run.
- `node examples/proof-of-purchase.ts deal.json --as buyer --receipt receipt.json` (or `--as seller`) runs that check with the chain read, which a proof of payment needs, then writes `deal.ledger.json` and `deal.proof.md` beside the file, with no network. The row holds the commitment ID, the settlement time, what was bought (the deliverable's description in the signed terms), the counterparty's address, the price, the adjudication fee, the operator fee and what you paid and received, each in base units and in the token's decimals, the outcome and the transactions. The page shows the order, the acceptance and the payment with their hashes, who signed what by address, the file's SHA-256 and the command that checks it again. Your own facts come only from you, as flags or a JSON file (`--facts`): `--name`, `--counterparty-name`, `--reference`, `--fiat-value`, `--fiat-currency`, `--rate`, `--rate-source`, `--rate-time`. Anything you leave out stays "not stated"; the command looks up no rate and computes no fiat value. It is not an invoice (the seller issues that) and not tax advice. Same deals as the check: closed by an accept or a reject, with no operator fee paid. Exit 0 written; 1 a check failed; 2 could not run, no `--receipt`, or a deal it does not cover. Only exit 0 writes anything.

## The seller's invoice

`node examples/seller-invoice.ts record.json seller.json [--buyer buyer.json]` writes the seller's invoice for a finished deal from its deal record (`examples/deal-record.ts`, `--as seller`), with no network. It reruns the record's offline checks, refuses an unfinished deal or a failed check by name, and writes `<number>.json` (stable field names) and `<number>.html` (a page to print or send) beside the record, or in `--out <directory>`.
- Released: an invoice for the price. Settled: an invoice for your share. Refunded, or the refunded part of a settlement: a credit note, only with `--credits <invoice number> --credits-date <YYYY-MM-DD>`; with no earlier invoice, nothing is due. A price of 0: nothing is due from the seller.
- `seller.json` is yours: `{"format": "crow-seller-details/1", "name", "address": [lines], "country", "taxId", "lines": [lines], "email", "tax": {"treatment": "rate", "rateBps": 1900} | {"treatment": "reverse-charge"} | {"treatment": "none"}, "numbering": {"invoice": {"prefix": "INV-2026-", "next": 1}, "creditNote": {...}}}`, with an optional `note` in `tax`. The command adds `issued`, its register: a number is used once, a series never skips, and a rerun on the same deal writes the same document. Reverse charge needs both parties' `taxId`.
- `buyer.json` holds what the buyer told you: any of `name`, `address`, `country`, `taxId`, `reference`. A tax rate splits the tax out of the price, since the buyer paid the price. A fiat figure needs `--rate <decimal> --currency <code> --rate-source <text> --rate-date <date>`.
- A deal on an earlier deployment: set `HOLDBACK_DEPLOYMENT` to that deployment's JSON from `contracts/deployments/`.
- The command decides no tax rate or treatment and gives no tax or legal advice: the seller issues the document and answers for it. Exit 0 written; 1 refused; 2 could not run. Only exit 0 writes anything.
